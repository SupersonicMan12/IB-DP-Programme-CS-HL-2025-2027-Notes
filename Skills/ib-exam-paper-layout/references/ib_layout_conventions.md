# IB Diploma Programme examination layout conventions (all subjects)

Use these when laying out supplied content. They describe how IB papers *look*, not what to put in them.
If the user's material contradicts something here (for example a subject-specific format they
supply), follow the user.

## 1. Question paper

**Cover page**
- Subject (bold, large), then level ("Higher level" / "Standard level"), then paper name
  ("Paper 1", "Paper 2 — Extended-response question (practice)"), then an optional title.
- Duration (real paper) or suggested time (practice material).
- **Instructions to candidates** as bullets. Common lines:
  - "Do not open this examination paper until instructed to do so."
  - "Answer all questions." / "Answer all of the questions from one of the options." /
    "Section A: answer all questions. Section B: answer one question."
  - Calculator lines (maths/sciences): "You are not permitted access to any calculator for this
    paper." / "A graphic display calculator is required for this paper."
  - Booklet lines: "Answers must be written within the answer boxes provided." / "A clean copy of
    the formula booklet is required for this paper."
  - Last bullet: "The maximum mark for this examination paper is **[N marks]**."
- For practice material, an optional note saying what the paper covers. Never present practice material
  as an official IB paper or invent official paper codes.

**Every later page**
- A centred page number "– n –" in the header.
- "**Turn over**" bottom right on every page except the last.
- The last page may end with "**End of examination paper**" (or "End of Option A", etc.).

**Questions**
- The question number ("3.") hangs in the left margin and all question content is indented under it.
- Mathematics and many science papers put **[Maximum mark: n]** flush right above each question.
- Parts are labelled (a), (b), (c) …; sub-parts (i), (ii), (iii) …. Where a part has sub-parts,
  "(a) (i)" appear on the same line.
- **Marks for each answerable part go flush right in square brackets**: [2]. The marks sit on the
  last line of the part's text.
- Shared information (stimulus) goes *between* parts, just before the parts that use it, never inside
  a part. Parts that build on each other say "Hence …", "Using your answer to part (b) …".
- If a question runs over a page: "(Question n continues on the following page)" at the bottom and
  "(Question n continued)" at the top of the next page. Break at logical points. IB papers leave
  white space rather than splitting a figure or a part across pages.
- Sections: "**Section A**" heading followed by its instruction ("Answer **all** questions …").
  Section B often starts on a new page.
- Question–answer booklets (maths, some sciences): dotted answer lines or blank space under each part,
  roughly 1–2 lines per mark.

**Figures, tables, sources**
- Captioned **above**: "**Figure 1**: …" or "**Table 2**: …". Centred.
- Number figures/tables in order through the paper and refer to them by number in the text.
- Tables: bold header row; boxed data cells; units in the headers.
- Sources (e.g. for data, images, extracts) in small type, right-aligned, under the figure:
  "Source: adapted from …".
- Diagrams that aren't accurate are labelled "diagram not to scale" (maths).
- Extracts / text passages (languages, humanities): set off in a box or indented block, with line
  numbers if questions refer to lines.

**Typography**
- A sans-serif body (Arial-like), about 11 pt. Variables in italics ($x$, $R$), units upright.
  Use proper symbols: × not x, − (minus) not -, ≤ ≥ ≠ °.
- Code, identifiers and file names in a monospace font.
- Bold is for structure (headings, **all**, **not**, **one**), not for hints.

**Command terms** (IB glossary, used in sentence case at the start of a part): Analyse, Annotate,
Apply, Calculate, Comment, Compare, Compare and contrast, Construct, Contrast, Deduce, Define,
Demonstrate, Derive, Describe, Design, Determine, Discuss, Distinguish, Draw, Estimate, Evaluate,
Examine, Explain, Explore, Hence, Hence or otherwise, Identify, Interpret, Investigate, Justify,
Label, List, Measure, Outline, Plot, Predict, Prove, Show, Show that, Sketch, Solve, State,
Suggest, Summarize, To what extent, Trace, Verify, Write down. If supplied wording uses a
non-IB verb, keep it as written but point it out to the user.

## 2. Markscheme

**Front matter**
- Cover: "Markscheme", subject, level, paper, total marks.
- General marking instructions (supplied by the user, or copied from their past markschemes).
- Optional abbreviation table (maths) and summary table (part | marks | skill | syllabus).

**Body**
- One heading per question ("Question 3"), then each part as a bold heading row with its mark total
  flush right: "(b)" … "[3]".
- Optional intro: "Award **[1]** for each of the following:" / "Award **[2 max]**."
- Mark points, one per line, with the mark flush right.

| Subject family | Point style | Mark notation |
|---|---|---|
| Sciences, Computer science, Business, Economics, Geography … | dash bullet, the point ends with ";" | [1] after each point; "[2 max]"; "/" separates alternatives; OWTTE = "or words to that effect"; ECF = "error carried forward"; words in (brackets) are optional; <u>underlined</u> words are required |
| Mathematics (AA / AI) | no bullet; one line of working per line | M1 (method), A1 (accuracy/answer), R1 (reasoning), N marks, AG (answer given, no mark), (M1)/(A1) implied, FT follow-through; several on one line: "M1A1" |
| Essay-based (Paper 2/3 in humanities, TOK-like) | level descriptors table (marks band → descriptor) plus indicative content bullets | "Marks 7–9: …" |

- Code or long worked answers go in a framed block labelled "Example answer" / "Example solution".
- *Notes for examiners*: smaller bullets after the points (accepted alternatives, common errors and
  the mark they lose, caps, follow-through rules).
- Maths markschemes end each question with "**Total [n marks]**" flush right.
- Optional final section, "Notes for teachers (not part of the markscheme)".

## 3. Consistency checks the layout must pass
- The sum of part marks = each question's [Maximum mark] = the markscheme part totals = the paper total
  on the cover. (The renderer warns when these disagree.)
- Part labels are in sequence with no gaps; sub-parts restart at (i) for every part.
- Every figure/table referred to in the text exists, and the numbering is in order.
- Markscheme parts line up one-to-one with question parts (same labels, same marks).
- No page ends with an orphaned heading, a caption separated from its figure, or a part label
  separated from its text.
- "Turn over" isn't on the last page; continuation notes are only on pages where a question actually continues.
